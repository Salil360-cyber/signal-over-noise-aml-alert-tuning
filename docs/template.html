Signal Over Noise

Five monitoring rules ran over 4.49 million synthetic bank transfers. Most alerts were noise. Use the tools below to see which rules earn their place, what a stricter threshold costs, and how far a risk score can shrink the review queue.

Tool 1

Build your own rule package

Switch rules on or off and pick the structuring threshold. Counts are exact: they come from the combination of rules that fired on every alerted account-day.

Tool 2

Move one threshold

Pick a rule and drag its threshold. The chart shows the trade-off across every tested value: fewer alerts, but fewer suspicious account-days caught.

Tool 3

Review the riskiest alerts first

Two models trained on 1–7 Sep alerts score the unseen 8–10 Sep alerts (55,641 of them). Drag to choose how much of the queue analysts review, highest score first.

Random order finds true positives in proportion to the work: review 30%, find about 30%.

Coverage check

Which laundering patterns do the rules catch?

The dataset ships a file of known laundering patterns. For each pattern, this is the share of its account-days that at least one rule alerted on.

No pattern is well covered. Overall the five rules alert on 25.3% of suspicious account-days, so the bigger gap is coverage, not just alert volume. The pattern file lists a subset of laundering transactions (3,209), so these shares are indicative.

Data: IBM synthetic AML dataset HI-Small (Altman et al., NeurIPS 2023). All accounts and transfers are simulated. Window 1–10 Sep 2022 after removing a thin tail of mostly laundering rows. One alert = one account on one day.
